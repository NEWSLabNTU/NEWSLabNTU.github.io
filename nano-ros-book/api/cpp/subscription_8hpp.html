phase-456 W1 — the same registration, with the callback's CAPTURE held by the ARENA rather than by the caller.

The difference is the lifetime, not the dispatch. ..._register above passes context through untouched, so the caller must keep whatever it points at alive AND unmoved for as long as the executor runs — which is why a callback-style entity was immovable after registration, and why the returning create_subscription had to heap-allocate a cell to give a capturing lambda a stable address.

Here the first capture_len bytes at capture are COPIED into the arena entry and the dispatch context becomes the entry's own copy. Nothing of the caller's is referenced after the call returns, so the caller may keep nothing at all — which is what lets the C++ object become a handle.

capture_len must not exceed NROS_CPP_CALLBACK_CAPACITY; a longer capture is rejected rather than truncated, and the C++ side asserts the same bound at compile time so that never reaches the runtime.
